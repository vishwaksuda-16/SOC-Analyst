# Investigation 2: Business Email Compromise (BEC) - CEO Impersonation Phishing Campaign

## Executive Summary

**Case ID:** SOC-2024-002  
**Date:** June 20, 2024  
**Investigator:** Vishwak Sudarsanan  
**Severity:** CRITICAL  
**Status:** Confirmed Phishing - BEC Campaign

A sophisticated Business Email Compromise (BEC) phishing campaign targeted the Finance department, impersonating the CEO to authorize fraudulent wire transfer of $50,000. Email authentication checks failed (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), confirming sender spoofing. 15 employees received the malicious email. No confirmed wire transfers executed.

## Evidence Collection

### Email Headers Analyzed

**From Field (Spoofed):**
```
From: ceo@company.com
```
- Easily spoofed
- Claims legitimate sender
- Not validated by authentication

**Return-Path (Real Sender):**
```
Return-Path: <no-reply@company-secure.tk>
```
- CRITICAL: Different from claimed sender
- Uses attacker-controlled domain (company-secure.tk)
- "company-secure" mimics "company" (typosquatting)

**Received Headers (Email Path):**
```
Received: from mail.attacker.com (mail.attacker.com [203.0.113.45])
  by company-mail.company.com with SMTP
Received: from suspicious-server.ru (suspicious-server.ru [198.51.100.99])
  by mail.attacker.com with SMTP
```

**Path Analysis:**
1. Email originated: suspicious-server.ru [198.51.100.99]
2. Relayed through: mail.attacker.com [203.0.113.45]
3. Arrived at: company-mail.company.com

**Conclusion:** Email originated from attacker infrastructure, NOT company servers

**Authentication Results (All Failed):**
```
spf=fail (sender IP [203.0.113.45] does not match SPF policy for company.com)
dkim=fail (signature verification failed)
dmarc=fail (DMARC policy requires alignment)
```

| Auth Type | Result | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **SPF** | FAIL | Sender IP not authorized to send from company.com |
| **DKIM** | FAIL | Email digital signature doesn't match company's cryptographic key |
| **DMARC** | FAIL | SPF+DKIM alignment failed; email policy rejects this message |

**Conclusion:** All three authentication mechanisms confirm email is SPOOFED and NOT from actual CEO account

## Email Header Analysis (Technical)

### SPF (Sender Policy Framework) Explanation

**Purpose:** SPF prevents email spoofing by checking if sender IP is authorized to send from claimed domain

**What SPF Says:**
```
spf=fail (sender IP [203.0.113.45] does not match SPF policy for company.com)
```

**Translation:**
- Email claims to be from: company.com
- Actual sender IP is: 203.0.113.45
- company.com's SPF record says: "Only email from [OUR AUTHORIZED SERVERS] are valid"
- 203.0.113.45 is NOT in that list
- **Verdict:** Email is spoofed

---

### DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail) Explanation

**Purpose:** DKIM adds digital signature to email proving it came from legitimate domain

**What DKIM Says:**
```
dkim=fail (signature verification failed)
```

**Translation:**
- Email claims to be from: company.com
- Email includes cryptographic signature
- company.com's public key is used to verify signature
- Signature verification FAILS
- **Verdict:** Email was NOT signed by company.com's private key (attacker can't fake DKIM)

---

### DMARC (Domain-based Message Authentication Reporting and Conformance) Explanation

**Purpose:** DMARC policy says "If SPF OR DKIM fails, reject the email"

**What DMARC Says:**
```
dmarc=fail (DMARC policy requires alignment)
```

**Translation:**
- SPF failed (✗)
- DKIM failed (✗)
- DMARC policy: Reject if BOTH fail
- **Verdict:** Email should be REJECTED (not delivered or marked as suspicious)

## Detection Opportunities

### What Worked
- ✅ Email security gateway flagged authentication failures
- ✅ User vigilance (employee questioned authenticity)
- ✅ Finance staff familiar with legitimate CEO processes
- ✅ No prior similar emails from CEO (anomaly detected)

### Detection Gaps
- ⚠️ Email gateway allowed delivery (some orgs would auto-quarantine failed auth)
- ⚠️ No real-time phishing alert system (user had to manually report)
- ⚠️ Typosquatting domain not in threat feed
- ⚠️ No outbound link tracking (don't know if anyone clicked)

### Recommendations for Future Prevention
1. Configure email gateway to auto-quarantine emails with failed SPF/DKIM/DMARC
2. Implement user phishing reporting button (easy escalation)
3. Add CEO name to sensitive words list (monitor emails claiming to be from CEO)
4. Implement outbound link click monitoring
5. Establish verification procedure for high-value wire transfers (require phone call)
6. Implement domain similarity detection (alert on lookalike domains)
7. Train Finance staff on BEC tactics and verification procedures
8. Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) for financial system access
